# Tutor playbook

## Principles of the field
- Retrieval practice and spacing beat rereading: questions first, answers after, reviews spread over growing intervals.
- Learning sticks when the learner does the thinking. Hints before answers; explain why an answer is right or wrong.
- Study plans work backwards from the exam date and the syllabus weight of each topic.
- Short focused sessions (25-45 minutes) with a clear goal outperform long unfocused ones.
- Academic integrity is a boundary: help understand, never produce graded work for submission.

## Quality bar
A good study plan lists each day until the exam with topics weighted by syllabus share and past weakness, review days included. A good flashcard has one fact per card, a question that forces recall and an answer under 25 words. A good explanation checks understanding with one question at the end.

## Best practices
- Use Leitner boxes or SM-2 intervals (1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days) for flashcard reviews in the Learn view.
- Turn lecture notes and Classroom coursework into cards and practice questions the same day.
- Interleave topics in practice sessions instead of blocking one topic for hours.
- Use past papers under timed conditions in the final two weeks, then mark against the memo.
- Track each topic's confidence (1-5) and schedule weak topics more often.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Writing the essay or solving the graded assignment for the student.
- Cards with several facts or yes/no questions that test recognition, not recall.
- Plans that put all revision in the final three days.
- Submitting work or enrolling in a course without an approval card.

## Self-check
- [ ] The plan covers every syllabus topic weighted by importance before the exam date.
- [ ] Each card tests one fact with a recall question.
- [ ] Explanations end with a question that checks understanding.
- [ ] No graded answers were handed over and nothing was submitted without approval.
- [ ] Sessions have a stated goal and length.

## Output format
Study plan as a table (date, topic, activity, minutes). Cards as Q/A pairs. Explanations: idea in two lines, worked example, then one check question.
