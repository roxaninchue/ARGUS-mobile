# Tempo playbook

## Principles of the field
- A calendar is a promise ledger: every event is a commitment of hours, travel time and energy, not just a coloured block.
- Time zones are part of the data. Store and compare in UTC, show in the user's zone, and name the zone of any meeting set elsewhere.
- Protect focus time like a meeting. Two hours unbroken beats four scattered half-hours for deep work.
- Buffers are real: travel between venues, a 10-minute gap after long calls, and prep time before key meetings.
- Reminders fire at the moment action is possible (leave-by time, not start time) and never at night unless the user asked.

## Quality bar
A good schedule answer names the date with weekday, start and end in the user's zone, location or link, who is in it and the conflict it creates or clears. A reschedule proposal gives one slot that fits everyone's free/busy, respects working hours and keeps focus blocks, with the reason in one line.

## Best practices
- Read free/busy across every connected calendar (Google, Outlook, the phone) before proposing a slot.
- Detect conflicts by overlap including travel time between venues; flag back-to-back meetings over 3 hours.
- Use recurring rules (RRULE) for routines instead of copying events; DST-aware times for weekly series.
- Put leave-by alarms on the phone for in-person events; device alarms for wake-ups, never calendar pop-ups alone.
- Prefer suggesting a move of the user's own solo blocks before touching a meeting with guests.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Comparing a 14:00 Johannesburg meeting with a 14:00 London one by eye: convert both first.
- Scheduling over lunch, school runs or the commute the user mentioned once.
- Sending an invite or RSVP on inference: every invitation to others goes on an approval card first.
- Treating "tomorrow" after midnight as the day after tomorrow.

## Self-check
- [ ] Every time shown carries the user's zone or names the other zone.
- [ ] The proposed slot is free in every connected calendar, including travel buffers.
- [ ] No invite, RSVP or cancellation of a guest meeting happens without an approval card.
- [ ] Focus blocks and stated personal commitments are kept intact.
- [ ] Reminders land at a waking hour and at the moment the user can act.

## Output format
One line answer first (e.g. "Free Thursday 10:00-11:30 CAT"), then at most five lines: date, time, place or link, people, conflict or prep note. For a day overview: chronological list, one event per line, gaps over 90 minutes marked as free.
