# Studio playbook

## Principles of the field
- Creative work starts from the audience and the single message; every headline, image and cut serves it.
- Brand consistency builds recognition: the same colours, type, logo use and voice across posts and pages.
- Drafts beat descriptions. Show a version quickly, then iterate on the user's reaction.
- Each platform has its own format: aspect ratios, caption length, hook in the first second or line.
- Originality and rights matter: use the user's own assets, licensed stock or generated images, and credit sources.

## Quality bar
A good social post has a hook in the first line, one message, a clear call to action, platform-correct size (1080x1350 for feed, 1080x1920 for stories and reels) and alt text. A good design follows a type scale, a grid and enough contrast (WCAG AA). A good script fits the target length with timing marks.

## Best practices
- Keep a brand kit: colours in hex, fonts, logo files, tone words and banned phrases.
- Write three headline or hook options and pick one with a reason.
- Plan a content calendar with themes per weekday and posts drafted a week ahead.
- Export images in the platform's size and compress under its upload limit; keep the source file in the Library.
- Hand heavy visual work to the designer worker and long copy to the writer worker.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Posting or publishing anything publicly without an approval card.
- Text-heavy images that are unreadable on a phone.
- Copying another creator's artwork, logo, music or voice.
- Captions that bury the call to action after the "more" cut.

## Self-check
- [ ] The draft has one message and a hook in the first line or second.
- [ ] Sizes, lengths and formats match the target platform.
- [ ] Colours, fonts and voice follow the brand kit.
- [ ] Nothing was posted or sent to a client without an approval card.
- [ ] All assets are the user's, licensed or generated, with credits where needed.

## Output format
The draft itself first (image, copy or script). Then one line on the choice behind it, the platform specs used, and the next step (post via approval card, or revise).
